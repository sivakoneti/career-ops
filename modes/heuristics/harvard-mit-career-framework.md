# Harvard MCS & MIT CAPD Career Framework Guide

Synthesized core principles and operational playbooks from the Harvard FAS Mignone Center for Career Success (MCS), Harvard Extension School (HES), and MIT Career Advising & Professional Development (CAPD).

---

## 1. The Inverted Job Search & The Hidden Job Market (MIT CAPD & Harvard MCS)

- **The Asymmetry**: Traditional job seekers search from the "Top Down" (Job boards → Career Fairs → Direct Outreach → Internal Network). Employers hire from the **"Bottom Up"** (Internal promotion/transfers → Employee Referrals → Trusted Personal/Professional Network → Direct Sourcing → Job boards as a last resort).
- **The Golden Rule**: Meeting and connecting with professionals in your target field is the single **most effective method** of securing high-impact technical roles. Relying solely on portal submissions places your application at the lowest priority tier.

---

## 2. Networking & Connection Architecture (Harvard MCS)

### A. Core Etiquette & Mindset
- **"Mutual Benefit" Philosophy**: Never treat outreach as begging or wasting someone's time. Informational outreach is mutually beneficial:
  - **For the Contact**: Identifies high-caliber future colleagues, strengthens their internal standing (referral bonuses in tech/finance/consulting), and provides fresh industry/competitor insights.
  - **For the Candidate**: Gathers insider intelligence, team pain points, unadvertised headcount, and establishes advocates before the interview process begins.
- **The "Never Ask Directly for a Job" Rule (Non-Recruiters/Peers/Alumni)**:
  - Do NOT ask a peer, engineer, or alumni for a job directly. Ask for **insights, trajectory advice, or how their team tackles specific technical challenges**. 
  - Let them make the natural offer to refer you once rapport and competence are demonstrated.
  - *Exception (Direct Referral Ask)*: When reaching out to an **assigned recruiter** or a contact with an **established prior working relationship / warm history**, direct, concise asks are expected and respected.

### B. High-Conversion Outreach Structure
1. **Context & Common Thread**: How you found them (shared tech focus, mutual interest, company milestone).
2. **Value & Proof Point**: Concrete credentials that establish instant credibility (e.g., years in enterprise systems, cleared benchmarks, specific technical scale).
3. **Low-Friction Asynchronous CTA**: Offer a short, bounded time frame (10–15 mins) or a low-friction question rather than demanding an open-ended call.

### C. Systematic Tracking & Cadence
- **Persistence Window**: People are busy; lack of immediate response is rarely a rejection.
- **Cadence**: Follow up after **5–7 business days**. Max **2 follow-ups**. If no response after 3 total touchpoints, politely rotate to another contact at the same firm.
- **Relationship Maintenance**: Send immediate thank-you notes after conversations and provide career milestone updates periodically to build enduring advocates.

## 3. Resumes & Impact Framing (Harvard HES / FAS)

- **Action-Verb & Result Formula**: Every bullet must pair an active verb with context and quantifiable business/technical impact:
  $$\text{Action Verb} + \text{Context / Problem Solved} + \text{Method / Technology} + \text{Quantifiable Outcome}$$
- **Avoid Passive & Fluff Language**: Remove personal pronouns ("I"), narrative fluff, and generic buzzwords.
- **Targeted Skimmability**: Recruiter attention spans average 6–10 seconds. Format with clear visual hierarchy, strong reverse-chronological structure, and prominent technical proof points.

---

## 4. Offer Evaluation & Negotiation Strategy (Harvard MCS)

- **Total Compensation Focus**: Evaluate base salary, equity vesting schedules, sign-on bonuses, remote flexibility, and career trajectory rather than base pay alone.
- **Negotiating for Time**: The most common and valuable first negotiation move is asking for a decision window extension (typically 1–2 weeks) to synchronize competing offers.
- **Collaborative Negotiation**: Always negotiate over live audio/video when possible; research industry compensation benchmarks thoroughly; articulate value-add; maintain unwavering professionalism and never issue ultimatums.
